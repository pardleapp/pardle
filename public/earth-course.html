The course

A long par 72 that gives back on the par 5s

The Earth Course has hosted every edition of this event since 2009, so the record is unusually clean: one layout, one week of the year, and a field that never shrinks, because there is no cut. Every player who tees it up on Thursday plays all four rounds, which means round-to-round comparisons here are not muddied by half the field going home on Friday night.

The card measures 7,706 yards for a par of 72. Length is real, but the scoring is lopsided. The four par 5s (2, 7, 14 and 18) average 0.31 under par and yield a birdie or better 39% of the time. They make up a fifth of the holes and produce 38.5% of all birdies. The par 3s average 0.04 over par, and the long two-shotters at 9 and 12 are the hardest holes on the course.

The bars matter as much as the dots. The 18th has the widest swing on the course: in its easiest round it played 0.68 under par, in its hardest 0.22 over. The drivable-looking 15th ranges from 0.83 under to 0.07 over without its tee moving more than nine yards, so that swing is pins and wind. The 9th is the hardest hole on average and has produced the hardest single round of any hole, at 0.62 over par.

Set-up

The course shrinks as the week goes on

The Tour's shot-tracking feed records the yardage each hole actually played every day. Four years of it (2022–2025) tell the same story every time: the course plays its longest on Thursday and its shortest at the weekend. On average it measured 7,657 yards on Thursday and 7,491 on Sunday, a drop of 166 yards.

Almost all of that comes from four holes. The 18th is the big one: it has averaged 633 yards in round one and 552 on Sunday, an 81-yard move that turns a three-shot par 5 into a genuine eagle chance. The 14th comes forward about 22 yards, the par-3 13th about 20, and the par-3 4th about 12. The other 14 holes barely move.

The scoring follows the tape measure, with one twist. Thursday is clearly the hardest day, at 0.78 under par per player against 1.46 under on Friday, 1.84 under on Saturday and 1.38 under on Sunday. Saturday, not Sunday, is the easiest day, because Sunday pressure and Sunday pins claw some of the shorter set-up back. Across the four tracked years, each extra 10 yards on a hole was worth only about 0.02 of a shot, so the yardage is not the whole story. The 18th is the exception: it plays 0.28 shots easier on Sunday than on Thursday, the largest single change anywhere on the course.

Angle. First-round scoring markets should be priced off a tougher course than the event average. If you are pricing round leaders or round-score lines from a whole-week scoring average, Thursday at the Earth Course is the round to fade the low numbers.
The finish

Where the tournament is decided

The closing stretch is the most volatile four-hole run on the course. The spread of players' scores across holes 15 to 18 is 1.41 shots (standard deviation), against 1.19 to 1.25 for every other four-hole block. Two holes drive it. The 18th is the most volatile single hole on the property: three eagles in every 100 attempts, and a double bogey or worse in two. The 16th is the most dangerous: it yields a double bogey or worse 3.2% of the time, the highest rate on the course.

That volatility has not produced late comebacks from distance, though. In all 17 editions the eventual winner was either leading or one shot back after 14 holes of the final round. The leader at that point went on to win 11 times. After 54 holes the leader (or co-leader) won 10 times, and no winner has started Sunday more than three shots back. Two editions went to a play-off: 2010 and 2025.

Angle. The in-play value is with the two or three players at the top through 14 holes on Sunday, not with chasers. Expect around three players within two shots at that point. The 18th's Sunday numbers (47% birdie or better, 4% eagle) mean a one-shot deficit on the 18th tee is very live; a three-shot deficit has never been closed here.
Pins

The Sunday pins barely move

The shot-tracking feed also stores every pin position. Plotting four years of them on the real green outlines shows something useful: the final-round flags go in almost exactly the same spot every year. On the 1st, 7th, 11th, 13th and 18th, the four Sunday pins sit within about a metre of each other. On the 6th, 8th, 10th, 16th and 17th they are within about five metres. Across all rounds, pins on the same hole are typically 12 to 20 metres apart.

So the Sunday set-up is known in advance. The 18th flag goes middle-left. The 7th goes front-right, the 11th back-right, and the 13th front-right. The 10th's Sunday pin sits at the front. That flag and the 18th's are the most generous Sunday pins on the course, each playing about 0.12 shots easier than the hole's long-run average.

The toughest flags show up in the same places too. The back pin on the 18th is the single hardest pin-day we have: it played 0.38 shots harder than the hole's average for that week in round one of 2023, and it has been used again on Friday in 2024 and 2025 (both about 0.2–0.3 harder). Back pins on the 9th, the hardest hole on the course, add another 0.2–0.3. A front-right pin on the par-5 2nd played 0.34 shots harder than that week's average in 2022.

Angle. If the 18th goes back on Thursday or Friday, with the tee at its longest, the hole that usually hands out birdies can play close to par. Watch the hole-location sheet: it is the clearest single-hole tell we found.
Weather

A hot, dry run-in and a windy start

The four weeks before the event (10 October to 7 November) were the hottest we can find in the last 18 years at the course: average daily highs of 36.6°C, against a previous range of 34.3–35.8°C, and only two light showers totalling about 1.5mm. That points to firm, quick conditions, but the history says not to lean on it. Across 17 editions, the heat of the run-in has had no measurable link to how the course scored (correlation 0.13). The Earth Course is irrigated and overseeded, so it plays to the green-keepers' plan more than the weather's.

The forecast for the week is the bigger story. Event weeks here are usually calm: across every past round, the wind during playing hours averaged 7.7 mph. The usual pattern is a light south-easterly land breeze in the morning that swings to a north-westerly sea breeze around midday and builds to about 10 mph by late afternoon.

This Thursday is forecast to be different. Four weather models put the playing-hours average at 13–17 mph from the west-north-west, once each is adjusted to the same scale as the historical data. The windiest round in 17 years averaged 13.1 mph (round one, 2019). The direction is the normal afternoon one; what is unusual is that it is already blowing at 10–12 mph when the first group tees off, instead of the usual calm morning. Friday stays fresh, then it eases over the weekend.

Does wind actually make the Earth Course harder?

Less than you would think. Across 68 rounds, each extra mph of average wind has added about 0.08 shots per player per round, and the relationship is weak (the error on that estimate is 0.05). The two windiest rounds on record were not the hardest: round one in 2019 (13.1 mph) averaged 0.96 under par, and round one in 2023 (12.8 mph) averaged 0.30 under. The wide fairways and big greens absorb a lot.

Direction matters more at hole level. When a hole plays into the wind its scoring average rises, and the effect is largest on the par 5s, where it decides who can reach in two. The model below estimates each hole's sensitivity to headwind from 17 years of round averages, shrunk toward the course-wide figure so that one odd day cannot dominate a hole.

Put Thursday's forecast in (about 14 mph from 300°, west-north-west) and the tool adds around 0.6 shots per player to a normal Thursday. The holes that turn into the wind are the 4th, 18th, 1st, 8th and 10th: that means a 240-yard-plus par 3 that is already the second-hardest hole, and the 18th at its full Thursday length. Downwind, the 5th, 7th, 9th and 16th play with the breeze, so the par-5 7th gets a helping wind and the 9th, normally the hardest hole, loses some of its teeth.

Angle. Thursday's 18th, into the wind at over 630 yards, is unlikely to be reachable for most of the field. Players who rely on the par 5s to score lose more on Thursday than the course average suggests, and first-round under/over lines set off normal conditions look low.
Tee times

Is there a wave edge?

With 50 players going off one tee in twos, the first-round draw spans about four and a half hours, roughly 8:15 to 12:40. Round two is drawn on round-one scores, worst first, so the leaders always go out last on Friday. To compare early and late starters fairly, we measured each player against their own scoring in rounds that did not decide that day's draw.

The late half of the draw has scored 0.28 shots worse than the early half on Thursday and Friday, and it has been worse in 60% of rounds. That is within the noise (the error is 0.19), and it does not grow on days when the afternoon breeze builds more. Two archived years (2017 and 2018) had round-one tee sheets that were really round-two draws, so they are left out.

This Thursday tilts things slightly further. The leading model has the morning at about 12 mph and the afternoon at 16. At 0.08 shots per mph, that is roughly another 0.3 shots against the late starters, on top of the small historical lean. Combined, call it half a shot per player in favour of the morning, enough to matter in first-round leader and two-ball pricing and not much else. The draw is not yet published; check who lands in the last hour on Thursday.

Who it suits

A course that stretches the gap between good and great

A useful way to describe a course is its slope: if one player is rated a shot per round better than another, how many shots does he actually beat him by at this venue? We measured it at 35 regular DP World Tour venues using pre-tournament skill ratings, comparing like with like by taking only the 60 highest-rated players at each event.

At the Earth Course the answer is 1.12 shots, against 0.84 at the typical venue. That ranks 6th of 35, alongside Wentworth, Valderrama and Gary Player CC. Better players gain more than their rating here. The error is 0.11, so the gap to the typical venue is about two and a half times the noise.

The winners' list says the same thing in plainer terms. Rory McIlroy, Jon Rahm and Matt Fitzpatrick have three wins each; Henrik Stenson has two. Where pre-event ratings exist (since 2017), the winner was the 1st, 2nd, 2nd, 3rd or 6th best-rated player in the field five times out of seven. The exceptions were Nicolai Højgaard (14th, 2023) and Danny Willett (53rd of 60, 2018).

The type of player is less clear than the class of player. Long hitters do well here because the par 5s carry so much of the scoring and Sunday's 18th becomes reachable. But Fitzpatrick, never one of the longest, has won it three times, and Stenson twice. What the course rewards most is elite iron play into big, well-defined greens, then making the birdies the par 5s offer. Fitzpatrick is not in the field this year.

Vs expected asks a sharper question: how many shots per round better (positive) or worse (negative) than their pre-tournament skill rating predicted has each player scored here? Ratings exist from 2017, so it covers fewer rounds than the raw figure; the number of rounds is shown in brackets, and anything built on four or eight rounds is mostly noise.

McIlroy's record here is remarkable even by his standards: 15 starts, three wins, ten top-fives and an average finish of 6.4, beating the field by more than two shots a round. Most of that is simply class: against his own rating he has been only 0.22 a round better here since 2017. Rahm is better still in a smaller sample: five starts, three wins, never outside the top five, and an average finish of 2.4, and he has beaten his rating by 1.67 shots a round over 20 rounds. Tommy Fleetwood has four top-fives in 13 visits without a win. Matt Wallace (two top-fives in five starts) and Adrián Otaegui, who lives in the UAE and has outplayed his rating here over 20 rounds, are the best course-history profiles further down the board.

On the other side, Aaron Rai has averaged a finish of 45th in four starts and has played more than a shot a round below his rating here, and Francesco Laporta's two visits ended 45th and 46th. Race to Dubai leader Eugenio Chacarra has one start, a 37th. Course history is noisy, so treat these records as a nudge rather than a verdict, but in a 50-man field with no cut, a small edge on a player at a big price goes a long way.

Summary

The betting angles

Round 1

Thursday is the hard day, and this year harder still

The course plays longest on Thursday and has scored 0.6 shots per player worse than Sunday. Add a forecast 13–17 mph wind, beyond anything seen in the event, and first-round under/over lines and winning-score-of-round markets built from whole-week averages look too low.

Draw

A small morning edge on Thursday

Historically the late half plays about 0.3 shots worse; Thursday's breeze is forecast to build by around 4 mph. Together, roughly half a shot per player to the early starters. Worth a nudge in first-round leader and three-ball prices.

Class

Respect the top of the market

A slope of 1.12 (6th of 35 venues) and a winners' list of McIlroy, Rahm, Fitzpatrick and Stenson both say the best players separate here. Long-shot outright value is harder to find than at a typical event.

In-play

Back the leaders through 14 on Sunday, not the chasers

Every winner was leading or one back with four to play; none started Sunday more than three back. The 15–18 stretch is the most volatile on the course, so a one-shot gap is live, but a three-shot gap has never been closed.

Hole 18

Watch the 18th's tee and pin

Sunday's 18th plays 81 yards shorter than Thursday's and 0.28 shots easier, with a middle-left pin used every year. A back pin earlier in the week can turn it into a par hole. It is the biggest single-hole swing in the data.

Wind

Into the wind: 4, 18, 1, 8, 10

With the forecast west-north-westerly, the long par-3 4th and the 18th play into it; 5, 7, 9 and 16 play downwind. Par-5-dependent scorers lose more than usual on Thursday.
